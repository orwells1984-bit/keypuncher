# External toddler apps: what Keypuncher can take from them (October 2026)

A read-only review of three small toddler web games, done on 2026-10-25, for ideas that could improve
Keypuncher. Nothing from them was run or copied: none of the three repositories carries a licence
file (sofi-games' README says MIT, but there is no LICENSE in the tree), so every result below is an
idea to re-implement in Keypuncher's own way, never code to lift. Each clone was read from a
disposable folder outside the project (`P:\ClaudeScratch\repo-review-keypuncher\<repo>`), with no
page opened in a browser and no build script run.

| Repo | Commit read | Ages | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `RazHadas/sofi-games` | a7a2be3 (2026-10-23) | 1 to 3 | Hebrew HTML5 toys for a tablet in kiosk mode |
| `vanu270/vaanyas-playground` | 6e4074c (2026-07-27) | 3 to 7 | English HTML5 learning games with a progression engine |
| `madebydia/airplanes.games` | e95e6f4 (2026-06-27) | 3 to 5 | HTML5 games; only `SPEC.md` was read |

Keypuncher facts the cards are checked against, from `README.md` and the code at main 269e320:

- **Reflex budget.** A key or tap is answered in the same frame (a 15 ms timer in `PlayPage`); no
  model and no file or network work on that path. `MashGrouper` joins keys within 40 ms and makes the
  third a burst; `SpawnLimiter` holds new things to the calm level's rate (0.7, 1 or 2 a second);
  bursts are at least 0.7 s apart; a letter's word waits 0.9 s for a pause.
- **Art Rules** (`ArtStyle`, `CalmLimits`). Baby: 4 live things, 1 moving, bodies at least 0.18 of the
  short screen side across, outlines at least 0.018, five flat saturated colours in near-black ink, at
  most 4 dots in a field, no stripes. Toddler and Big toddler loosen each number. Nothing finer than
  the minimum stroke is drawn at all; every hue snaps to the palette; the backdrop keeps the level's
  brightness in every scene (`SceneGround`); there is no night except the goodnight; and
  `ArtRulesTests` fails any patch of the screen that flashes more than three times a second.
- **Grown-up gate** (`GrownUpGate`). Ctrl+Shift+Q, or Esc held 3 s, shows a four-letter word at the
  top left; typing it within 20 s opens the grown-ups' page. Keys keep working for the child meanwhile.
- **Windows lockdown.** `KeyboardHook` keeps every key from Windows while the toy is in front (Win,
  Alt+Tab, Alt+F4, Ctrl+Esc, media keys; not Ctrl+Alt+Del or Fn) and reads the Icelandic layout;
  `AccessibilityShortcuts` turns the Sticky, Filter and Toggle Keys hotkeys off for the run;
  `MauiProgram` uses the borderless full-screen presenter; `ToyApp` swaps pages in place, so there is
  no back button or navigation bar. Touch: `PlayPage` takes presses only (`SKTouchAction.Pressed`),
  counts fingers, and three or more make a burst; finger moves are ignored.
- **Sound** (`Mixer`). 48 kHz, 24 voices; a note is one oscillator (sine, triangle, square, saw or
  noise) through a one-pole low-pass, with a linear attack and an exponential decay; speech ducks the
  notes; a limiter and a 0.8 volume cap sit on the output, with a 3 s ramp at start. Animal noises are
  synthesised, not recorded (`AnimalCalls`), with gentler versions for babies. The voice patch
  (`Voice`) is part of the schema the model fills (`RecipeSchema`), so a new waveform touches the
  director as well.

## Card 1: sofi-games

**What it does.** Six full-screen touch toys for a 1.5-year-old on a tablet, one HTML file each:
animal cards with recorded noises, bubbles to pop, a six-key pentatonic piano, a firework on every
tap, finger painting with a tone per finger, and a stick-building camera prototype (not read).
Everything gives positive feedback; there is nothing to get wrong. A tap score and all-time best sit
at the top of every game. The background follows the wall clock (night, dawn, morning, afternoon,
sunset, evening). Kiosk lockdown is partly in the pages and mostly left to the OS (Android app
pinning, `chrome --kiosk` or Assigned Access on Windows, Guided Access on iPad).

**Files read.** `README.md`, `index.html`, `theme.js`, `manifest.json`, `score.js`, `build.js`,
`bubbles.html`, `piano.html`, `fireworks.html`, `draw.html`; `animals.html` skimmed (399 KB, almost
all base64 OGG recordings generated by `build.js`).

### (a) Touch lockdown, and what each technique means on Windows

| Their technique | Why they do it | On Keypuncher's Windows build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewport `user-scalable=no`; `touch-action: manipulation` on the page and `touch-action: none` on canvases | Stops double-tap and pinch zoom in the browser | Nothing to do: a SkiaSharp canvas in a native window has no zoom. |
| `touchstart` and `touchmove` with more than one finger cancelled; `gesturestart` cancelled | Chrome on Android ignores `user-scalable=no`; Safari pinches anyway | Same: no browser, no pinch. A long press on glass becomes a right-click, and nothing is bound to it. |
| `contextmenu` cancelled, `user-select: none`, tap highlight off | No menus or selection under a mashing palm | Already the case: the play page has no text or controls. |
| `history.pushState` trap on the back button | The Android back button leaves the page | Covered by design: `ToyApp` swaps pages in place, there is no navigation stack, and a PC has no back button. |
| A fullscreen button, with fullscreen re-entered on the first touch of the next page because browsers demand a gesture | Hide the browser and the OS bars | Covered, and better: the full-screen presenter needs no gesture. One thing to check on the touch laptop: whether a swipe from the top edge reveals the window's title bar and close button. |
| The way out is a 64 px home button at 35 % opacity, held for 700 ms, with a ring that fills during the hold | Keep a toddler from leaving by accident | Keypuncher's typed word is far stronger (card 2 compares them). The ring is the one thing worth taking: a quiet cue while Esc is held, so the grown-up can see the 3 s are being counted. |
| Edge swipes | Not handled; left to the OS kiosk mode | **A gap today.** On a Windows laptop with a touch screen, a swipe from the left edge opens Task View, from the right the notification centre, from the bottom the taskbar. Each puts another window in front, and `KeyboardHook` only keeps keys while the toy is in front, so the next bang on the keys goes to Windows. See the recommendation at the end. |
| Touchpad gestures | Not a tablet concern | **The bigger gap on a laptop.** The touchpad sits right under the keys an infant bangs. Three- and four-finger taps and swipes on a precision touchpad open search, Task View or the notification centre, or switch desktops, all before any hook sees them. Pinch and two-finger scroll do nothing in the canvas. |
| Wake lock | Not used anywhere in sofi-games, nor in the other two | **Missing in Keypuncher as well.** A child who pauses, or the goodnight's rest, lets the display sleep after the power plan's timeout, and if Windows asks for a sign-in on waking, the next press meets the lock screen. A display request while the toy is in front is a few lines in `Keypuncher.Platform.Windows`. |

### (b) Activities, against the reflex budget and the Art Rules

- **Bubbles popping on a tap.** Ten to eighteen translucent bubbles drift up on their own, one more
  every 1.2 s; a tap on one pops it into 14 small particles with a short descending pop. Two things do
  not fit. The bubbles appear without the child doing anything, and Keypuncher's premise is that a
  thing appears because the child pressed (`SpawnLimiter`, and Baby's four live things). And a bubble
  that is 55 to 85 % transparent with a 2.5 px rim fails the outline and contrast rules at every
  level. What does fit is the response to a touch on something already there: Keypuncher wakes it
  (`World.Touch`), and a pop *sound* for that moment is a cheap improvement (see (c)). Popping a
  thing away would also undo the persistent landscape the child is building. **Skip** the activity;
  **adapt** the sound.
- **A paint trail under a finger.** The best-fitting touch idea in the three repos. Each finger draws
  a 22 px round-capped stroke whose hue drifts, leaves a dot even for a single tap, sheds sparkles,
  and holds a soft triangle tone whose pitch follows the finger up and down the screen; the paint
  fades over about 40 s so the screen never fills. In Keypuncher terms: a trail role in `Entity`
  holding a polyline, appended on `SKTouchAction.Moved` in `PlayPage` (which ignores moves today) and
  drawn by `SceneRenderer` as a stroke at least `MinStroke` wide (at Baby that is 0.018 of the short
  side, about 19 px on a 1080-row screen, so sofi's 22 px was already in the right range), in a
  palette colour with the level's ink outline under it, no glow (a glow is a soft edge, which the
  rules forbid), settling to `HistoryOpacity` over `SettleSeconds` like everything else. The tone
  needs one thing the `Mixer` lacks: a held voice with a release, since every voice today decays on
  its own. Budget: appending a point and stroking a path is well under a millisecond. Rules: a trail
  never flashes, and its colour snaps. Must not break: the three-finger burst (trails only for the
  first two fingers; a third ends them), `World.Touch` waking things under the finger, and the saved
  world (trails are not saved, or saved as history only). Only touch has this; a keyboard has no
  equivalent, which is why it ranks below the keyboard ideas. **Experiment**, medium effort, only if a
  touch screen is in regular use.
- **One firework per tap.** 45 to 65 sparks with trails, gravity and drag, plus a white flash ring and
  a boom with crackles. Against the Art Rules this is the worst fit: a white flash on every press is
  exactly the luminance transition the flash test counts, and sparks of 2.5 to 5 px are far below
  `MinStroke` and `MaxDots` (4 at Baby). Keypuncher's answer to a mash is already one burst at least
  0.7 s apart (`BuiltInRecipes.Burst`, a dot field capped by `MaxDots`). What could carry over, at Big
  toddler only, is the sparks' *motion*: gravity, drag and a short trail on the burst's dots, within
  `MaxDots` (12) and with no flash ring. **Skip** at Baby and Toddler; **experiment** (low) at Big
  toddler for the motion.
- **The piano.** Six full-height keys, one note each, multi-touch by pointer capture. Keypuncher is
  already this with a hundred keys; the transferable parts are the patch (below) and the confirmation
  that major pentatonic (they use C D E G A C) is the right scale for mashing. Nothing to change.
- **Time-of-day backgrounds from the wall clock.** `theme.js` picks a palette by the hour and
  transitions over 60 s. Keypuncher's `Scene` already has a time of day (dawn to evening) that the
  director only moves forward, and no night. The idea worth taking is the *seed*: at `StartSession`,
  set the scene's time of day from the clock (dawn before 7, morning to 11, midday, afternoon,
  evening from 17), so the sky agrees with the window, then let the director move it on as today.
  `SceneGround` keeps the level's brightness whatever the hour, so contrast holds, and night stays
  the goodnight's alone. Must not break: the director's forward-only rule within a session
  (`Scene.Next`, which already wraps evening to the next dawn), and the scene fade (a retime at
  session start should go through `SetScene`, so it is never a jump). **Adapt**, small.
- **Tap score and all-time best.** Numbers at the top of the screen, bumped on every tap. Keypuncher
  has no score on purpose: an infant gets nothing from it, and it is the kind of clutter the Art
  Rules keep off the screen. **Skip.**
- **Animal cards.** Swipe to change card, tap for a recording. Swiping is a gesture beyond the child;
  recordings are the road the toy chose not to take (`AnimalCalls` makes its own). One practical
  confirmation: they pre-create every audio object to remove the first-play delay, which is what
  `AnimalCalls.Warm` and `SpeechLibrary.PreloadAsync` already do. **Skip.**
- **`build.js`, `manifest.json`.** A Node script that inlines OGG recordings as base64; a PWA
  manifest. Not applicable to a native app. One detail inside `build.js`: the pig's oink is
  synthesised (a sawtooth that sweeps up and back down, twice), the kind of recipe `AnimalCalls`
  already uses for everything else. There is no pig in `Shape` today, so nothing to do until there is.

### (c) Synth patches, for `Keypuncher.Audio`

- **Piano note.** Triangle at the fundamental plus sine at 2x and 3x at 0.45, 0.15 and 0.06; an 8 ms
  attack; a fast fall to half level by 150 ms and a long tail to 1.6 s. Keypuncher's `SynthVoice` is
  one oscillator with a single exponential decay, which is cleaner but thinner. Two harmonics and a
  two-stage decay (a fast drop, then a long tail) would make the key note warmer at no measurable
  cost: three oscillators per voice at 48 kHz is nothing. It can be done inside the existing Sine and
  Triangle rendering with no schema change, or as a new waveform, which would also touch
  `RecipeSchema` and `RecipeValidator`. Must not break: headroom (the harmonics add level, so scale
  back to the single-oscillator peak before the limiter), the per-wave low-pass cutoffs, and
  `AudioTests`. Listen with `tools/Keypuncher.Preview`, which writes a WAV of a scripted session.
  **Adapt**, small; try it on a branch first.
- **Pop.** A sine swept from 900 Hz down to 150 Hz in 120 ms, gone by 180 ms. The one patch with a
  clear slot: the cue when a touch wakes a thing, and possibly the dead-key sparkle. `SynthVoice` has
  no pitch envelope; a sweep (a start ratio that glides to 1 over a few tens of milliseconds) is a
  small, schema-neutral addition as long as only the built-in effects use it, because the model's
  `Voice` cannot ask for it. The same sweep gives the firework's thump and the pig's oink, so one
  mechanism serves three uses. **Experiment**, small.
- **Firework boom and crackle.** A sine from 120 Hz down to 45 Hz over 0.35 s, plus five to eight
  30 ms bursts of white noise through a band-pass at 2.5 to 5.5 kHz, scattered over the next
  50 to 400 ms. A boom is what `GentleCalls` exists to avoid: **skip** at Baby and Toddler. At Big
  toddler the crackle could go with the mash chord, but it needs a band-pass noise voice
  (`SynthVoice` only has a low-pass), so medium effort for a small gain. **Experiment**, low priority.
- **Drawing tone.** A triangle at 0.07 gain, pitch 220 to 660 Hz by finger height, glided with a 30 ms
  time constant, 50 ms in and 60 ms out. Only useful with the trail, and it needs the held voice
  described there.

**Verdict for the repo.** Adapt three small things (the clock-seeded time of day, the warmer note
patch, the pop sweep); experiment with the trail if a touch screen matters; skip the activities that
spawn on their own, score or flash. **Effort:** small for the three adaptions, medium for the trail.
**Must not break:** the reflex budget (every adaption here is on the audio thread or at session
start, not in the key path), the Art Rules (no translucency, no glow, no white flash, no particles
under `MinStroke`), and the grown-up gate (nothing here touches it).

## Card 2: vaanyas-playground

**What it does.** A learning playground for 3-to-7-year-olds: letters, counting, animals, maths,
science, memory and logic quizzes, plus an adventure mode with seven worlds unlocked by stars,
levels and XP, daily challenges, stickers, achievements, a fox mascot that talks, and a parent
dashboard of minutes per day, skills and accuracy. Speech is the browser's `speechSynthesis` with a
ranked voice pick. An adaptive engine looks at the last eight answers per skill and makes the next
question gentler or harder, and shows the hint at once to a child who is struggling.

**Files read.** `parents.html`, `core.js`, `common.js`, `sw.js`, `letters.html`, `counting.html`,
`animals.html`, `data.js`, `manifest.json`; `index.html` and `world.html` searched for how the parent
page is reached.

### The parent gate

There is none. `parents.html` is a plain "For Parents" link on the front page, and the only barrier
on the dashboard is a browser `confirm()` on "Reset progress". For a 3-to-7-year-old on a web page
that is a defensible choice; for an infant it is no gate at all. The three side by side:

| | vaanyas-playground | sofi-games | Keypuncher |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The way out | A link on the menu | Hold a small dim button for 700 ms | Ctrl+Shift+Q, or Esc held 3 s, then type the shown four-letter word within 20 s |
| A palm or a lean | Opens it | Opens it: a resting palm holds 700 ms easily | Shows the word; the toy keeps playing; nothing opens |
| A curious two-year-old | Opens it | Learns the hold in a day | Cannot type the word |
| Feedback to the grown-up | None needed | A ring fills during the hold | None until the word appears |
| Needs | A finger | A finger | A keyboard |

Keypuncher's gate resists a toddler better than either. Two notes. First, the one thing sofi does
better is the filling ring: a quiet mark while Esc is held tells the grown-up the hold is being
counted (`GrownUpGate` already tracks when Esc went down; the page could draw a small corner arc). A
toddler who sees it fill still cannot type the word, so it gives nothing away. **Adapt**, tiny.
Second, the gate needs a keyboard, which is the crux of the tablet question flagged at the end: on a
touch-only device there would be no way to the grown-ups' page at all.

### Sequencing and feedback, against the "if the child pauses, a word follows" rule

- **Letters.** Their free play answers a key or an on-screen letter with everything at once: the big
  letter in a random colour, the picture, the caption, and one utterance, "K! K is for Kite".
  Keypuncher says the letter's sound at once and lets the word wait 0.9 s for a pause, with its own
  picture; a later letter replaces the pending word, and a mash or digit drops it. For an infant the
  pause rule is the better sequence: one thing at a time, paced by the child; theirs is a
  three-year-old's sentence. A two-part phrase ("K, like kite") is a possibility for a later level
  and would need recordings in all three languages, so not now. **Nothing to change.**
- **Counting.** Their counting page is the one real idea here. The things bounce in, and the *child*
  taps each one, hearing "one", "two", "three" as they go, each tapped thing lighting up, before any
  question is asked. Keypuncher counts a digit's things on its own at 0.55 s intervals. Child-paced
  counting is one-to-one correspondence, the real pre-maths skill, and it fits Big toddler: after a
  digit has made and counted its things, a touch on one of them says its number again (and wakes it,
  as a touch already does). `Entity` would carry the count index; `ToyEngine.OnTouch` would check
  it. Must not break: counting is cancelled by other input today and should stay so; the speech
  channel plays one clip at a time, and a tap mid-count must not cut the count off. Mouse clicks
  already count as touches, which is fine. **Experiment** at Big toddler, small to medium.
- **Adaptive difficulty.** Their engine moves the difficulty from the last eight answers and shows
  hints sooner to a struggling child. Keypuncher has no questions, so the only analogue is pacing:
  the word delay could follow the child's recent gaps between presses (a slow "looker" hears the
  word sooner, a hammerer later). The counter-argument wins for an infant: the toy's value is that
  the same press always does the same thing, and a delay that drifts breaks that. **Skip** for now;
  the calm level, set by the grown-up, is the right adaptive control at this age.
- **Praise, "Try again", stars, streaks, XP, stickers, achievements, the mascot.** All rewards
  outside the play itself, for a child old enough to want them. An infant's reward is the thing that
  appeared; sofi's own principle ("no wrong taps") is Keypuncher's too. **Skip.**
- **Facts spoken to the child** ("Cow gives us milk"). Keypuncher's rule is the opposite: the model's
  names and facts are for grown-ups only, and the child's words come from the concept list. **Skip**,
  and worth keeping as a deliberate contrast.
- **Parent dashboard.** Minutes played per day for a week, counted in 15 s ticks while the page is
  visible. Keypuncher already knows play time (`ToyEngine` keeps it, excluding idle time); writing a
  daily total to the data folder and showing the last seven days on the grown-ups' page would cost
  little and help the owner choose a session length. No child-facing change. **Experiment**, small,
  low priority.
- **Reduce motion, high contrast, night mode.** CSS filters and animation kills. Keypuncher does these
  structurally (`CalmLimits.Moving`, `MaxMotionHz`, the `ArtStyle` colours, the goodnight's dimming),
  which is better than a filter that would shift every colour the Art Rules tests pin down. **Skip.**
- **`core.js`, `common.js`, `sw.js`, `data.js` otherwise.** A progression engine, voice ranking for
  `speechSynthesis` (it prefers English neural voices), Web Audio chimes, confetti, a service worker
  for offline use, and content tables. Not applicable to a native toy with recorded voices; the
  content is English only.

**Verdict for the repo.** One experiment (child-paced counting at Big toddler), one tiny adaption (a
hold cue for the Esc exit), one low-priority experiment (play minutes on the grown-ups' page); the
rest is for older children. **Effort:** small. **Must not break:** the gate (the cue must not open
anything or shorten the typed-word step), the counting cancellation rules, the speech channel.

## Card 3: airplanes.games (`SPEC.md` only)

**What it does.** Five aeroplane games for 3-to-5-year-olds, specified on one page: trace a landing
path; complete a pattern (AB, AABB, ABC, ABA) by dragging the right plane into a slot; sort planes
into hangars, with colour hints that disappear at higher levels; steer a plane through a maze with
on-screen arrows or the keyboard's arrows and WASD; count cargo boxes that bounce in one by one and
tap the number. Rules for every game: daytime theme only ("NO dark"), large tap targets, a home
button top left with no gate, confetti on completion, Web Audio sound effects, wrong answers shake
and say "Try again!", and levels advance after three correct in a row.

**Files read.** `SPEC.md`, and `README.md` for the age range.

**For Big toddler and later.** Nothing here is for the current child, but the spec is a tidy picture
of the step after Big toddler, and four things are worth writing down now:

- **Counting that ends in a question.** Cargo Count is Keypuncher's digit behaviour (things arrive
  one by one and are counted) followed by "how many?" with two or three big digits to choose from.
  In Keypuncher that would be the first time the toy asks the child anything: a three-year-old
  presses the digit key that matches. A future level above Big toddler, not Big toddler itself.
- **Keys that move a thing.** Flight Path takes arrow keys and WASD. Every key in Keypuncher *makes*
  a thing; at three, the arrow keys could *move* the newest creature, a keyboard-native mechanic the
  touch-first apps cannot offer and the first step from "my press makes" to "my press steers". The
  first idea for a level above Big toddler.
- **Scaffolding that fades with level.** Logic Hangar colour-codes the cards at levels 1 and 2 and
  drops the colour at 3 and 4; levels advance after three correct in a row rather than through a
  settings page. That is the calm-level ladder's own logic (bigger, bolder, fewer at Baby; more at
  Big toddler), and a reminder that any future question mode should start with a hint on screen and
  take it away later.
- **Daytime only.** Their design rule agrees with Keypuncher's "the goodnight is the only night".

**Verdict:** skip now; keep the first three items for a level above Big toddler. **Effort:** none now.
**Must not break:** nothing today; a question mode later would have to keep "no wrong presses" at
every level below it.

## Touch lockdown on the Windows build: recommendation

Keypuncher already has, natively, everything the three web apps fight the browser for: a borderless
full screen with no gesture needed, no navigation stack, no zoom, no menus, and a keyboard hook that
swallows the system keys. What it lacks is on the two input surfaces a Windows laptop adds, and
neither is a keyboard. First, the **touchpad**, which sits right under the keys: three- and
four-finger taps and swipes on a precision touchpad reach the shell before any hook, open search or
Task View or the notification centre, and once another window is in front the hook lets every key
through, so the baby is typing into Windows. The grown-up can turn those gestures off once in
Settings (Devices, Touchpad: three- and four-finger gestures set to nothing), and the toy should say
so, and show the current state, on the grown-ups' page; setting and restoring the per-user touchpad
values from the app, as `AccessibilityShortcuts` does for Sticky Keys, is a reasonable second step
once the registry values and when they take effect have been verified on the touch laptop. Second,
the **touch screen's edge swipes** (Task View, notification centre, taskbar), which the shell handles
before the app and which only the edge-swipe policy (`AllowEdgeSwipe` under the Edge UI policies, a
machine-wide, administrator-only setting that should be verified on this Windows version) or a kiosk
mode turns off; Windows 10 Home has no Assigned Access, so the policy is the practical route, and it
belongs in a grown-up's setup note and a read-only line on the grown-ups' page, never in a write the

**The open question, flagged and not decided.** Everything above assumes a PC with a keyboard, which
is the toy's premise (the Icelandic layout, the key families, the accent key). If a tablet or
touch-first target ever exists, the grown-up gate has no touch path at all today, and every
keyboard-native idea here (the hold cue, the arrow keys later) is beside the point; the trail and
child-paced counting would move to the top of the list, and a touch gate that a toddler cannot work
(for instance, two far corners held at once for three seconds, then a shown sequence of four shapes
tapped on screen) would have to be designed before anything else. Whether that target will ever
exist is the owner's call; this review only notes which ideas depend on it.
